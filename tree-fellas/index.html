The Tree Fellas approach

The whole tree, roots to canopy.

A tree that looks fine can be in trouble below ground, and a tree that looks rough can often be saved. Justin looks at the whole picture before anyone picks up a saw.

  1. 01

    Look closely

    Root flare, soil, trunk, limbs and canopy, plus what's around the tree: the house, the lawn, recent construction.

  2. 02

    Find the cause

    Yellow leaves or dead limbs are symptoms. The plan starts with what's actually causing them.

  3. 03

    The gentlest fix that works

    Proper pruning, crown thinning or a trunk injection before anything drastic. Removal only when a tree truly can't be saved.

    Oak wilt high-risk (Feb–Jun): don't prune oaksLower-risk months

    Why it matters: in Texas, the beetles that spread oak wilt are most active from February through June, and they're drawn to fresh cuts. That's why oaks are pruned outside that window, and every oak cut gets painted right away.

    Services

    Seven ways to keep trees thriving.

    The same seven services on Justin's card. Every job starts from the same question: what's best for the tree, and for the people living around it?

    Start here

    Prevention Plans

    Starts with a full health assessment of roots, trunk and canopy, then a season-by-season plan to catch problems early: oak wilt timing, watering, mulching and check-ins.

    Tree Pruning

    Proper cuts at the branch collar to remove dead, weak or crossing limbs and guide strong structure. Never topping.

    Last resort

    Removals

    When a tree is dead, dangerous or truly can't recover, it comes down carefully and cleanly. Always the last resort, never the first suggestion.

    Storm Damage

    Broken limbs, hanging branches and split trees made safe after North Texas storms, with cuts that give the tree its best chance to recover.

    Crown Thinning

    Selectively removing interior branches so light and wind pass through. Less weight on the limbs, more air for the leaves, and the tree keeps its natural shape.

    Trunk Injections

    Treatment delivered straight into the tree's water-conducting tissue, used for certain diseases, pests and nutrient problems once the cause is identified.

    Brush Control

    Clearing overgrown brush, vines and crowding undergrowth so the trees you want to keep get the light, water and airflow they need.

    Interactive · Do it right

    Drag to see why how you cut matters.

    Two of the most common ways trees get hurt in North Texas yards. Slide the handle to compare.

    ToppedProperly pruned
    • ✕Topping chops limbs to stubs. The tree loses much of its food-making leaves at once, the stubs decay, and the fast regrowth is weakly attached and prone to breaking.
    • ✓Proper pruning removes branches back to the branch collar or a healthy side branch, so the wound can seal and the tree keeps its natural shape.
    • ✓Crown thinning takes out select interior branches. Light and wind pass through, limbs carry less weight, and the tree still looks like itself.
    VolcanoMulch ring
    • ✕Volcano mulching piles mulch against the trunk. The bark stays wet, which invites rot, insects and roots that circle and strangle the trunk.
    • ✓A proper mulch ring is 2 to 4 inches deep, as wide as you can make it, and pulled back a few inches so the root flare shows.
    • ✓Why it helps: mulch keeps roots cooler and holds moisture through a Texas summer, and it keeps mowers and trimmers away from the bark.
